WEEK 4 · DAY 2

Abraham's Faith and God's Promise

Genesis 15:1–6 · Approximately 10 minutes

1. Scripture Reading

Read Genesis 15:1–6 in the New International Version (NIV).

Pay particular attention to God's promise to Abraham and Abraham's response of faith.

2. Historical & Biblical Context

Abraham, originally named Abram, had been called by God to leave his homeland and travel to a land God would show him (Genesis 12:1–3).

God had promised to make Abraham into a great nation and bless all the families of the earth through him. Yet Abraham and his wife Sarah remained without the promised child.

In Genesis 15, Abraham expresses concern about having no heir. God responds by reassuring him that His promise will be fulfilled.

3. Understanding the Passage

God begins by telling Abraham not to be afraid and reminding him that He is Abraham's protector and reward.

Abraham honestly brings his concern to God. He cannot see how God's promise of a great nation will happen when he has no son.

God directs Abraham to look at the stars and promises that his descendants will be beyond counting.

Abraham believes God's promise. His faith is counted to him as righteousness. This does not mean Abraham earned God's favor through his own accomplishments. It shows the importance of trusting God's word.

4. Key Verses

Genesis 15:1

God reassures Abraham that He is his protector and reward.

Genesis 15:5

God uses the stars to illustrate the greatness of His promise.

Genesis 15:6

Abraham believes God, and his faith is counted as righteousness.

5. Old Testament Connections

Genesis 12:1–3 – God's Original Promise

God first called Abraham and promised to bless all nations through him. Genesis 15 reinforces that original promise.

Genesis 17:1–8 – The Covenant Confirmed

God later confirms His covenant with Abraham and promises that he will become the father of many nations.

Psalm 105:8–11 – God's Faithfulness

The psalmist remembers God's covenant with Abraham, demonstrating that God's promises endure across generations.

6. New Testament Connections

Romans 4:1–5

Paul uses Abraham as an example of righteousness through faith rather than through human works.

Galatians 3:6–9

Paul explains that those who have faith share in the blessing promised to Abraham.

Hebrews 11:8–12

Abraham is remembered as an example of faith because he trusted God's promise even when fulfillment seemed impossible.

7. Prophecy & Fulfillment

Genesis 12:3 contains God's promise that all peoples on earth would be blessed through Abraham.

Galatians 3:8–16 connects this promise to the gospel and ultimately to Christ, through whom the blessing reaches the nations.

This demonstrates how God's promise to Abraham becomes part of the larger biblical story of salvation through Jesus.

8. Big-Picture Biblical Connections

God's promise to Abraham is a major turning point in the biblical story. Through Abraham's family, God develops His covenant people.

The promise continues through Israel's history and ultimately leads to Jesus Christ.

What begins as a promise to one man becomes part of God's plan to bring blessing and salvation to people from every nation.

9. KEY TAKEAWAY / LESSON

Faith means trusting God's promises even when we cannot see how they will be fulfilled.

Abraham did not have all the answers, but he trusted the One who made the promise. Our faith is not based on having every circumstance figured out. It is based on the faithfulness and character of God.

10. Reflection Questions

1. Why do you think Abraham was concerned about God's promise?

2. What does Abraham's response teach us about trusting God?

3. Is there an area of your life where you struggle to trust God's timing or promises?

11. Practical Application

Identify one situation in which you are uncertain about the future. Instead of allowing uncertainty to control your response, consider what Scripture teaches about God's faithfulness.

Practice trusting God's character even when you cannot see the outcome.

End of Today's Lesson

Take a moment to consider what you have learned and how you can carry this lesson into your daily life.
